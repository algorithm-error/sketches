Panel

A simple box with padding and a semi-transparent background, for grouping controls over a sketch canvas.

Basic

The background is a translucent tint of --background, so whatever sits behind the panel still shows through — here, a full control panel dropped over a sketch's canvas.

Horizontal

With horizontal the panel drops its fixed width and lays its children out in a wrapping row — for a toolbar strip floating across the top or bottom of a sketch instead of a column down one side.

Each child gets 17rem, which is exactly what a vertical panel gives its controls (19rem less the 1rem of content padding on each side), so a control measures the same in either orientation and a sketch can switch between them without re-tuning anything. Set an inline flex on a child where a group needs more or less. Drag handle, collapse and emboss work the same as in a vertical panel.

The panel is positioned, so its width follows from which offsets you set: give it left or right and it hugs its content; give it both and it stretches between them as a full-width strip. Either way the row wraps once it runs out of room.

Placement

placement="left" or placement="right" pins the panel to a top corner of the viewport, --offset (default 1rem) from both edges, instead of positioning it from the page. A left-placed panel puts its collapse button on the left, the way window controls sit on a mac, and collapses back to that same corner.

Fixed

fixed drops the header. With no grip there is nothing to drag the panel by and no collapse button, so it stays where the page put it — for a sketch embedded in a page, where a panel that can wander off or be dismissed is a nuisance rather than a convenience.

Centered

Add center and the panel takes both offsets itself, sitting in the middle of whatever it is positioned against — a toolbar strip centred over the canvas rather than pinned to one corner. Give it a top or bottom as usual; the horizontal placement is handled for you.

Both edges are held --offset (default 1rem) from the sides, and the panel is only as wide as its controls need. Once they no longer fit between those offsets it stops centring and spans the gap instead, wrapping as it always does — so a narrow window loses the centring rather than the panel.
