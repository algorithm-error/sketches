Flow bezier

FlowBezier (see common/flow-bezier.js) walks a point through a FlowField and turns the walk into a chain of cubic bezier segments. Each step becomes an anchor, and the two control points of that anchor sit on the field direction at that anchor, pointing backwards and forwards at distance handle. Control points are stored relative to their anchor.

Every demo below is driven by the JSON under it. Edit it and the canvas redraws. Coordinates are in canvas pixels. Where a demo has a dice button it lays out a fresh field under the same curve settings.

Beziers on a flow field

One curve per starting point. The red vectors are the field itself, sampled per cell; getWeightedAverageAt interpolates between the four surrounding cells, so a curve does not snap to the grid. A curve stops early when it walks off the field.

Three of the settings shape the field rather than the curves. cellSize is the grid step in pixels, so a smaller one samples the noise more finely. increment is how far the noise moves per cell, which sets how fast the field turns. spread is how far off east the field is allowed to point, in radians, which is what keeps these curves crossing the canvas instead of leaving it.

From a start to an end

Pass end as well as start and the curve stops being a free walk. It still steps through the field, but each step mixes the field direction into the direction of the end point, and the stride is whatever lands on the end point in the steps that are left. Both endpoints are exact whatever the field does, and step is ignored because the spacing follows from the distance and steps.

pull is how much of the field gets mixed in at most: 0 is a straight line, 1 is the field alone. The mix peaks in the middle of the curve and fades to nothing at both ends, so the curve leaves and arrives heading straight at its endpoints rather than swerving into them.

smooth()

Raw control points follow the field direction at their own anchor only, so a segment can kink where two neighbouring directions disagree. smooth() rotates each interior anchor's handles by a quarter of the angle difference between its neighbours, which pulls the joins into line. It mutates the curve in place, so the two versions below are two separate instances built from the same parameters.

debug()

Draws each segment's skeleton over the curve: filled dots are anchors, hollow dots are control points, lines connect a control point to its anchor. Anchors are shared between neighbouring segments, so every interior anchor gets two hollow dots.

Drag any dot to edit the curve in place. An anchor takes its two control points with it; a control point moves on its own, so the two sides of an anchor stop being mirrored and the join turns into a corner. Editing the JSON rebuilds the curve from the field and discards the drags.

FlowBezier.fromVertices()

Builds the same structure from a list of points instead of a field, so there is no walk and no field to sample. The tangent at a vertex points from its previous vertex to its next one; the first and last vertex use their single neighbour. handle sets how far the control points sit along that tangent, so raising it rounds the curve and lowering it tightens it towards the straight polyline.

Segments

curve.segments is the list of drawable pieces, one shorter than curve.points. Each holds {anchor1, control1, anchor2, control2}, where control1 is the forward handle of the first anchor and control2 is the backward handle of the second. Here each segment is drawn on its own, in its own colour, instead of calling render().

toSpline()

Returns just the anchor points, in order, dropping the control points. It reads them off curve.segments through Bezier.toSpline (see common/bezier.js): the first segment's start anchor, then the end anchor of every segment. That is the polyline the walk actually took, which is what you want for hit testing, measuring, or handing the curve to anything that takes plain vertices — splineToBezier included.
